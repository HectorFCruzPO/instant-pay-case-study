# Measurement notes

These notes document the figures used in the public Instant Pay case study. Sources are the project lead's confirmed launch timeline and baseline, and the internal InstantPay Impact Report dated October 28, 2026. Underlying reports are not published here.

## Adoption milestones

| Observation | Reported usage | Basis |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| December 2025 baseline at launch | 36% | Project lead confirmed the December usage baseline and December 28 launch date |
| End of January 2026 | 79% | First month after launch; monthly adoption among active 1099 nurses |
| September 2026 | 94% | Monthly adoption among active 1099 nurses |

The chart displays only these three reported observations. No intermediate months are estimated. The December baseline is a monthly usage baseline at launch, not a measurement of new-product usage on its first day.

- January minus December: 79 − 36 = **43 percentage points**.
- September minus December: 94 − 36 = **58 percentage points**.
- September minus January: 94 − 79 = **15 percentage points**.

The December population and reporting definition have not been independently reconciled with the 2026 active-1099 monthly definition. The progression is descriptive and spans the W-2-to-1099 transition. It is not a like-for-like experiment or a causal estimate.

An earlier 96% peak was mentioned in discussion, but is excluded because its reporting period and denominator were not supplied. The defined September observation is 94%.

Weekly Instant Pay-active nurses were also considered during planning, defined as nurses who submitted a timecard and used Instant Pay in the same week. That is a separate metric from the monthly adoption figures above.

## Engagement

Monthly hours worked per 1099 nurse increased approximately **29% between January and September 2026**, as reported in the internal analysis. This is a change over time, not an estimated treatment effect of Instant Pay.

## Retention

- **Cohort:** 1099 nurses whose first paid week was between December 28, 2025 and March 31, 2026.
- **Still active:** paid for work in any week from September 6 through October 18, 2026.
- **Regular users:** used Instant Pay in at least half of their worked weeks.
- **Occasional users:** used Instant Pay, but in fewer than half of their worked weeks.
- **Non-users:** never used Instant Pay.

| Segment | Retention | Ratio to non-users, using rounded percentages |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Regular users | 36% | 36 ÷ 16 = **2.25×**, approximately 2.3× |
| Occasional users | 45% | 45 ÷ 16 = **2.8125×**, approximately 2.8× |
| Non-users | 16% | Reference |

The public shorthand “approximately 2–3×” refers to these observed ratios. It is not a claim that using Instant Pay increases an individual's retention probability by that amount. Cohort differences, selection effects, and the relationship between continued work and opportunity to use the product limit causal interpretation. Higher usage frequency did not correspond to the highest retention.

## Public disclosure

Selected percentages are rounded. Absolute nurse counts, sample sizes, transaction counts, payment volumes, monetary amounts, and underlying internal exports are intentionally omitted. The case study does not attribute broader platform growth to Instant Pay.
